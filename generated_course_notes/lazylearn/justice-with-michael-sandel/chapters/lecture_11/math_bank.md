## Definitions And Objects

- \( F_A \): Aristotelian freedom, understood as realization of potential and proper fit.
- \( F_K \): Kantian freedom, understood as autonomy.
- \( S_L \): liberal or unencumbered self, free and independent, choosing its ends.
- \( S_N \): narrative or encumbered self, situated in inherited roles and histories.
- \( D_N \): natural duties, universal duties owed to persons as persons.
- \( D_V \): voluntary obligations, duties arising from promise, contract, deal, office, enlistment, or explicit undertaking.
- \( D_S \): solidarity obligations, obligations of loyalty, membership, and community not reducible to consent.
- \( G \): conception of the good or good life.
- \( R \): rights / fair framework of rights.
- \( J \): justice.
- \( M \): social meanings or shared understandings.
- \( P \): patriotism.
- \( C \): community or membership-bearing group.
- \( \prec \) or \( > \): priority relation between communities or obligations; use only when explicitly marked as contested.
- \( \lor \): “or” in liberal reduction claims, chiefly consent or reciprocity.

## Derivation Steps

1. Aristotle begins with the best way to live.
2. Therefore law and politics are tied to cultivating virtue.
3. Therefore freedom is tied to realizing one’s telos or proper fit.
4. Therefore person and role are internally connected.

1. Kant rejects grounding justice in any substantive conception of the good.
2. Therefore law should not embody or promote a particular way of life.
3. Therefore freedom is autonomy, not role-fit.
4. Therefore institutions should secure a fair framework of rights within which ends are chosen.

1. If the self is free and independent, then unchosen history and inherited status do not bind by themselves.
2. If only chosen commitments bind, then obligations arise either universally or voluntarily.
3. Hence the liberal taxonomy: \( D_N + D_V \).

1. Communitarians argue that actual moral experience includes loyalty, solidarity, and membership.
2. These obligations often cannot be traced to an act of consent.
3. Therefore the liberal two-part taxonomy is incomplete.
4. Hence the proposed third category: \( D_S \).

1. MacIntyre says human beings are storytelling creatures.
2. Therefore “What should I do?” presupposes “What story am I part of?”
3. Therefore the self is situated in family, city, nation, and inherited roles.
4. Therefore moral starting points include debts, inheritances, expectations, and obligations not chosen from scratch.

1. Liberal objection: many supposed obligations of membership can be redescribed as consent or reciprocity.
2. Example pattern: parent-child care becomes repayment of benefit; patriotism becomes civic reciprocity; political duty becomes chosen undertaking.
3. Therefore one may try to absorb \( D_S \) back into \( D_V \) or into liberal-compatible reciprocity.
4. This is why the lecture asks whether any case remains where loyalty has independent force.

1. Sandel presses for a harder test case.
2. A valid communitarian case must show loyalty competing with universal principle rather than merely expressing it.
3. Dan, Billy Bulger, and Robert E. Lee are introduced as such tests.
4. Therefore the live issue becomes whether \( D_S \) can outweigh or at least compete with \( D_N \).

1. Final communitarian expansion: if right cannot be detached from good, then justice may be tied to communal meanings.
2. Walzer states justice in terms of shared understandings.
3. But the segregation example shows bad traditions can also invoke shared understandings.
4. Therefore the unresolved problem is whether \( J(M) \) collapses into conventionalism.

## Notation Choices

- Use text-style symbolic notation rather than imported formal ethics notation; the lecture itself is classificatory, not algebraic.
- Keep subscripts mnemonic and sparse:
  - \( A \) for Aristotelian
  - \( K \) for Kantian
  - \( L \) for liberal
  - \( N \) for narrative
  - \( V \) for voluntary
  - \( S \) for solidarity
- Use \( \Longrightarrow \) for argumentative consequence or conceptual implication, not strict logical entailment.
- Use \( = \) only for stable lecture-level identifications or compressed definitions.
- Use \( \approx \) for near-identification where Sandel effectively equates two descriptions without formally defining them.
- Use \( \stackrel{?}{>} \) for contested rankings such as universal versus particular or family versus country.
- Use \( \not\Rightarrow \) when the lecture denies that one consideration automatically yields another.
- Prefer plain text operators inside math mode, e.g. \( \text{natural duties} \), \( \text{social meanings} \), because there is no native symbolic apparatus in the source lecture.
- Do not introduce quantifiers, modal operators, deontic symbols, payoff matrices, or decision-theoretic notation; the lecture does not warrant them.
- Do not mark anything as `[visible]`; no validated frame-backed mathematics survived.

## Uncertain Mathematics

- No equation is visually attested. Every item here is transcript-backed or a cautious standard reconstruction.
- \( \text{narrative self} \approx \text{encumbered self} \) is faithful in spirit but still a compression; Sandel uses both ideas closely, but not as a formal identity.
- The hierarchy displays \( \text{family} > \text{town} > \text{country} > \text{humanity} \) and \( \text{most universal community} > \text{more particular communities} \) are not endorsed conclusions. They are competing proposals raised in discussion and should remain visibly tentative.
- \( \text{consent} \lor \text{reciprocity} \Rightarrow \text{all particular obligations} \) is an objection-schema, not a result Sandel accepts.
- \( \text{justice} = \text{relative to social meanings} \) and \( \text{just society} = \text{faithfulness to shared understandings} \) should be explicitly attributed to Walzer, not presented as Sandel’s settled view.
- \( \text{tradition} + \text{situated identity} \not\Rightarrow \text{justice} \) is best used only in the closing evaluative section after the segregation clip, since it summarizes the final worry rather than a mid-lecture premise.
- Avoid formalizing the family, French pilot, Ethiopian airlift, Dan, Billy Bulger, and Lee cases into symbols beyond brief labels; the lecture uses them as judgment tests, not as a formal derivation system.
- The transcript glitch “apart” should be normalized to “a part of” before using the MacIntyre implication.
