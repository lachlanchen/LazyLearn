## Definitions And Objects

- `narrative conception of the self`
  The self is understood as situated in a history and capable of generating obligations of solidarity or membership not reducible to consent.

- `voluntarist conception of the self`
  The Kantian/Rawlsian picture in which obligations are anchored in choice, consent, reciprocity, or universal duties owed to persons as such.

- `obligations of solidarity or membership`
  Obligations claimed by history, identity, loyalty, or membership rather than by contract or explicit agreement.

- `universal duties`
  Duties owed to human beings qua human beings, irrespective of special ties.

- `relativist linkage of justice to the good`
  Justice tracks the values or shared understandings prevailing in a community at a time.

- `non-relativist linkage of justice to the good`
  Justice depends on the moral worth or intrinsic value of the ends that rights and institutions serve.

- `telos`
  Purpose, end, or defining point of a practice or institution; central for Sandel’s treatment of sex, marriage, and public recognition.

- `moral permissibility`
  Whether a practice is morally allowed or disallowed.

- `honor / recognition`
  The public standing or approval the state confers by recognizing an institution such as marriage.

- `neutrality`
  The attempt to settle rights questions without taking a stand on contested moral or religious views.

- `third position`
  The neutrality-driven option that the state should stop recognizing marriage altogether.

- `Goodridge`
  Massachusetts case used by Sandel to test whether same-sex marriage can be defended on neutrality grounds alone.

- `reflective equilibrium`
  Method of justification by moving back and forth between considered judgments about cases and general principles that organize them.

- `reasonable pluralism`
  Rawls’s idea that conscientious, reasonable persons may persistently disagree about the good life, morality, and religion.

- `liberal respect`
  Respect as abstention, bracketing, or non-engagement with comprehensive moral and religious commitments in political argument.

- `engaged respect`
  Respect as taking others’ convictions seriously enough to engage, contest, and learn from them.

## Notation Choices

- Use `\text{...}` for all displayed formulas in the chapter. The lecture’s formal payload is conceptual rather than symbolic in the usual mathematical sense.

- Use `\Longrightarrow` for argumentative consequence and `\stackrel{?}{\Longrightarrow}` or `\stackrel{?}{<}` for open questions or contested implications.

- Use `\neq` only where Sandel explicitly draws or clearly implies a distinction, especially:
  \[
  \text{moral permissibility} \neq \text{public recognition}
  \]

- Normalize `voluntrous` to `voluntarist`.

- Keep `telos` in italics or plain transliterated prose in running text, but preserve it literally in displayed formulas when needed:
  \[
  \text{telos of marriage}
  \]

- Prefer `respect_{\mathrm{liberal}}` and `respect_{\mathrm{engaged}}` only in working displays or schematic summaries. In the polished chapter, prose definitions may read better unless a compact contrast is needed.

- Attribute the “three partners to every civil marriage” formula explicitly to Chief Justice Margaret Marshall when it appears in prose or display.

- Treat `reflective equilibrium` as the canonical label for Rawls’s method; do not invent shorter symbols like `RE`.

- Keep `justice`, `the good`, `neutrality`, `honor`, `recognition`, `pluralism`, and `friendship` as named conceptual objects rather than abbreviations.
