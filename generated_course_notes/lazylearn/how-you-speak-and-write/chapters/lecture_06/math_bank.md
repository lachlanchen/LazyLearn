## Definitions And Objects
- `immersion`: the temporary spell in which the reader feels present inside the story world.
- `metaphor`: an implied comparison; the lecture explicitly uses Billy’s description and `ghost quiet` this way.
- `simile`: an overtly signaled comparison, as in `quiet as a ghost`.
- `mental simulacra`: vivid internal reconstructions of what the character experiences.
- `static symbols`: the raw medium of prose; marks on a page rather than direct audiovisual presentation.
- `sensory set`: taste, smell, touch, hearing, sight, and motion.
- `abstract association`: a nonliteral conceptual link such as `ghost` modifying `quiet`.
- `weak spell`: prose that is understood propositionally but not felt bodily or imaginatively.
- `cliché`: an overused image that demands little imaginative work from the reader.
- `unexpected connotation`: a fresh descriptive link, as in `stewed cherry dress`, that forces active inference.
- `reader participation`: the reader meeting the writer halfway in imaginative construction.
