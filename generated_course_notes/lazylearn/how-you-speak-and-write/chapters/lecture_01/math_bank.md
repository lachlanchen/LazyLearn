## Definitions And Objects
- `curse of knowledge`: inability to subtract our own knowledge and re-enter the reader’s ignorance.
- `audience model`: what the reader knows, does not know, and can reasonably infer.
- `common ground`: the shared situation that makes speech easier than writing.
- `feedback`: live conversational signals such as confusion, clarification requests, body language, and attention.
- `abstraction`: compressed insider terminology that may be efficient within a field but empty for outsiders.
- `example`: a concrete case that pins a general claim to a determinate referent.
- `generalization`: the compressed statement that tells us what the example is an example of.
- `mental image`: the sensory or conceptual object the prose should help the reader form.
- `non-compositional meaning`: the meaning of a familiar phrase need not equal the literal sum of its parts.
- `euphony`: sound quality of prose when read aloud.
- `metrical structure`: the beat-pattern or rhythm of prose.
- `sibilance`: concentration of `s`-like sounds that can make prose unpleasant if overused.
- `alliteration`: repeated initial sound used sparingly for a “spark of pleasure.”
- `informalization`: long cultural shift toward spontaneity, familiarity, and conversational vernacular.
- `LLM prose`: orderly, plain, and often clear, but typically generic and pastiche-like.
- `pattern extraction`: Pinker’s late-stage term for what large models do well with massive input.

2. Reject malice as the main cause.
3. Note that many intelligent, sincere writers still produce unreadable prose.
4. Identify the missing step: they do not know where the audience is coming from.
5. Conclude that the curse of knowledge is the primary mechanism.

2. Note that deixis and shorthand work because the context is jointly available.
3. Add that conversation supplies immediate correction through feedback.
4. Remove common ground and feedback, and we get writing.
5. Conclude that writing must build explicitly on the page what speech can borrow from the situation.
