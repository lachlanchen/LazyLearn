# LRE Books

Summary: Evidence quality for this run is weak (4/5 book websearch artifacts are empty; the only non-empty artifact is mostly noisy SERP/forum content), so recommendations are conservative and anchored to the profile’s explicit gaps: focus, validation, measurement quality, narrative clarity, and dark-side risk control.

## Recommendations
- Good Strategy/Bad Strategy — Richard Rumelt [high]
  - Theme: Flagship focus and coherent prioritization
  - Why fit: You have high execution breadth but fragmentation risk. This book’s strategy kernel (diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent actions) directly fits your 30-90 day need to choose 1-2 flagship initiatives and avoid spreading effort across too many threads.
  - 30d action: Produce a 1-page strategy kernel for your top 3 initiatives, score each against one north-star metric, then commit to one flagship and one secondary bet for the next 30 days.
- The Mom Test — Rob Fitzpatrick [high]
  - Theme: Validation before building
  - Why fit: Your dark-side risk is shipping-heavy but impact-light loops. This book gives a concrete interview method to get decision-grade user evidence before adding features, which directly reduces build-first bias and improves distribution fit.
  - 30d action: Run 10 user interviews for one flagship workflow, extract repeated pain statements, and define 3 falsifiable product hypotheses to test before shipping new major features.
- How to Measure Anything — Douglas W. Hubbard [high]
  - Theme: Evidence quality and decision confidence
  - Why fit: You identified evidence-blind decisions and failing artifacts as core bottlenecks. This book is a direct fit for turning ambiguous strategic questions into measurable signals and hardening your evidence pipeline with uncertainty-aware metrics.
  - 30d action: Define 5 decision-critical metrics (adoption, retention, outcome delta, proof artifact completion, identity confidence), set collection rules, and treat missing/zero-byte artifacts as automatic pipeline failures.
- Essentialism — Greg McKeown [high]
  - Theme: Context-switch risk reduction
  - Why fit: You are running many parallel product/research tracks; context-switch overload is a named dark-side risk. This book gives an operating model for strict tradeoffs and deliberate non-doing, which protects deep work and execution quality.
  - 30d action: Set a WIP limit of 2 active initiatives, create a weekly stop-doing list, and archive or pause at least 3 non-core threads for the month.
- Obviously Awesome — April Dunford [medium]
  - Theme: Positioning and narrative coherence
  - Why fit: You have strong technical breadth but narrative coherence risk for external stakeholders. This book helps define category, alternatives, and differentiated value so users/investors can quickly understand the core value path of your ecosystem.
  - 30d action: Create a positioning canvas for the flagship product (competitive alternatives, unique attributes, proof), then rewrite the landing page and README around one clear positioning statement and 3 evidence-backed claims.
