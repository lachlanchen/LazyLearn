## Definitions And Objects
- `prior consent`: the act of joining political society and taking on political obligation.
- `legitimate authority`: political or military authority constrained by non-arbitrariness and general law.
- `self-possession`: the thought that we own ourselves and therefore seem not to be disposable by government.
- `recruitment shortfall`: the practical problem Sandel uses to replace abstract Lockean discussion with a live policy case.
- `Civil War hybrid system`: conscription plus a buyout/substitute market.
- `all-volunteer army`: Sandel’s paid army; the lecture treats `volunteer` as partly misleading.
- `outsourcing`: hiring mercenaries or private fighters at the standing wage.
- `coercion objection`: market choice may not be truly free under severe background inequality.
- `civic-obligation objection`: military service may be the wrong kind of good to allocate by market exchange.
- `tainted consent`: consent undermined either by coercion or by lack of relevant information.
- `commodification objection`: certain goods are degraded or misvalued when treated as objects of sale.
- `Baby M contract`: payment to Mary Beth Whitehead in exchange for bearing a child and surrendering the child to the Sterns.
- `alienated labor`: Anderson’s term for labor diverted away from the ends pregnancy and childbearing rightly promote.
- `V_{\text{non-market}}`: the set of non-use modes of valuation named at the end of the lecture.

## Derivation Steps
**Locke: from consent to conscription**
1. Start with the taxation case already granted from the previous lecture.
2. Separate present individual consent from prior consent to join society.
3. Move from property to life as the harder test case.
4. Ask whether self-possession blocks conscription.
5. State Locke’s condition: authority must not be arbitrary.
6. Use the sergeant example to show command over life may fall within rightful authority.
7. Contrast this with taking the soldier’s money, which would be arbitrary.
8. Conclude that consent can ground broad authority so long as it remains non-arbitrary.

**Military-service markets: from options to objections**
1. Pose the recruitment problem in Iraq.
2. Enumerate the three policy responses.
3. Introduce the Civil War hybrid as the revealing intermediate case.
4. Extract the first criticism: pricing substitution may price human life.
5. Refine that criticism into a stronger claim about coercion under inequality.
6. Extend the same pressure to the all-volunteer army.
7. Open the second line of criticism: patriotism and civic obligation may be misdescribed by market language.
8. Step back and name the two objections side by side.

**Surrogacy: from contract to tainted consent**
1. Present the Baby M contract as a voluntary agreement for money.
2. State the contractarian defense: a deal is a deal.
3. Introduce the objection from missing information.
4. Specify the missing information: the surrogate cannot know in advance the strength of the maternal bond.
5. Generalize the point: consent can fail even without overt coercion.
6. Compress the court’s reasoning into `pre-birth consent \not\Rightarrow fully informed consent`.

**Surrogacy: from commodification to non-market valuation**
1. Shift from flawed consent to the thought that the transaction is dehumanizing.
2. Recast that worry as a commodification objection.
3. Use the court’s language about sale of a child or sale of a mother’s right.
4. Introduce Anderson’s claim that surrogacy can force parental love into alienated labor.
5. Generalize from one case to a wider class of goods not properly valued by use or profit.
6. Name the alternative modes of valuation collected in \(V_{\text{non-market}}\).

## Notation Choices
- Use `\Longrightarrow` for argumentative implication, not numerical equality.
- Use `\not\Rightarrow` when the lecture blocks a tempting inference.
- Prefer textual expressions inside math mode, e.g. `\text{coercion}`, `\text{consent}`, `\text{civic obligation}`, rather than single-letter abstractions.
- Use `\text{objection}_1`, `\text{objection}_2` locally within a section; reset numbering when the lecture begins a new pair of objections.
- Use `\begin{cases}` for policy menus and branching alternatives.
- Use `\to` only for staged progression or escalation, not for logical entailment.
- Use `V_{\text{non-market}}` for Anderson’s list of non-market modes of valuation.
- Keep money amounts as plain textual examples, not as variables to be manipulated.
- Keep `all-volunteer army` and `paid army` visually distinct when both are needed; the lecture treats them as related but not rhetorically identical.
- Do not introduce operator notation, set-builder notation, or symbolic quantifiers beyond what is needed for structural clarity.
