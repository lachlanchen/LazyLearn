# How You Speak and Write: Proposed Chapter Order

This is a future reorganization plan only.

No current pipeline, filenames, or generated notes are changed by this note.

## Core editorial idea

1. Start with speaking.
2. Then move into why writing is harder than speaking.
3. End with the capstone idea: `Writing Can't Be Taught`.

For this project, `Writing Can't Be Taught` refers to:

- `How to Write Strikingly Well (Lee Child Interview)` (`uHBKKvDbQaY`)

## Proposed order

### Part I. Speak First

1. `How to Speak`
2. `How to Speak So That People Want to Listen`
3. `The 3-2-1 Speaking Trick That Forces You To Stop Rambling!`
4. `How to Force Yourself To Speak Coherently`
5. `How to speak with meaning`
6. `Harvard Thinking: How to Tell a Story`

### Part II. Why Writing Is Harder Than Speaking

7. `Harvard Professor Explains The Rules of Writing — Steven Pinker`
8. `Cambridge Professor: Why All Writing Sounds the Same Now (Robert Macfarlane)`
9. `How to Show, Not Tell: The Complete Writing Guide`
10. `How to write descriptively - Nalo Hopkinson`
11. `How to build a fictional world - Kate Messner`
12. `The Mindset Behind An Unforgettable Novel (Amor Towles Interview)`
13. `Pulitzer Prize-Winner Explains His Writing Process — Richard Powers`
14. `How to write a bestselling novel with author Ken Follett`
15. `Five Tips for Writing Your First Novel—Brandon Sanderson`
16. `How to Write a Novel: My Proven 12-Step Process`
17. `How to Write a Book: 13 Steps From a Bestselling Author`
18. `Ocean Vuong Teaches the Art of Writing (NYU Professor)`

### Part III. Capstone

19. `How to Write Strikingly Well (Lee Child Interview)`

## Short rationale

- Part I builds verbal clarity, rhythm, and audience awareness first.
- Part II argues that writing demands more compression, structure, precision, and revision than speaking.
- Part III closes with the strongest anti-formula chapter, where writing becomes judgment, taste, and lived craft rather than a mechanical recipe.
