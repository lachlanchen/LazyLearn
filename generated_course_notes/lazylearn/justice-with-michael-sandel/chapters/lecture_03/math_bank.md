## Definitions And Objects
- `D`: a distribution or end-state of holdings.
- `J(D)`: justice-status of a distribution.
- `J_{\mathrm{acq}}`: justice in acquisition; whether initial holdings were obtained justly.
- `J_{\mathrm{tr}}`: justice in transfer; whether later exchanges arose through free consent.
- `S_{\min}`: the minimal state allowed by the libertarian view.
- `S_{\mathrm{forbidden}}`: the three main categories of illegitimate state action in this lecture.
- `\text{SelfOwnership}(p)`: the claim that person `p` owns himself or herself.
- `\text{Need}` and `\text{Desert}`: distinct evaluative categories; the lecture treats them as non-equivalent.
- `\text{Coercion}`, `\text{Theft}`, `\text{ForcedLabor}`, `\text{Slavery}`: ordered terms in the Nozick escalation.
- `\text{Preferred}` versus `\text{Worthier}`: recap distinction used against Mill’s qualitative defense of utilitarianism.

## Derivation Steps
Mill’s long-run defense of rights
1. Assume respecting rights tends to improve overall welfare in the long run.
2. Conclude that utilitarianism can appear to protect rights instrumentally.
3. Ask whether there could be exception-cases where violating rights improves outcomes even in the long run.
4. Ask whether long-run welfare is the right kind of reason anyway.
5. Use the organ-transplant example to separate outcome-based restraint from intrinsic respect for persons.

Libertarian entitlement view
1. Start with an observed distribution `D`.
2. Refuse to judge `D` by pattern or inequality alone.
3. Ask whether initial holdings were acquired justly.
4. Ask whether later transfers arose from voluntary exchange.
5. If both answers are yes, treat the resulting distribution as just.
6. If either answer is no, treat the resulting distribution as unjust.

Gates and Jordan numerical move
1. Use Gates’s wealth and earning rate to make utilitarian redistribution intuitively attractive.
2. Use Jordan’s one-year income to make a concrete redistribution policy numerically vivid.
3. Move from “they will hardly notice” to “the poor benefit greatly.”
4. Then block that utilitarian inference by appealing to entitlement and rights rather than aggregate welfare.

Nozick’s taxation-to-slavery chain
1. Taxation takes part of a person’s earnings.
2. Earnings are the fruits of labor.
3. Taking the fruits of labor is morally akin to claiming part of the labor that produced them.
4. Claiming part of a person’s labor is forced labor.
5. Forced labor implies that the state is, in part, owner of the person.
6. Partial ownership of the person is slavery or a denial of self-possession.
7. Therefore redistribution by taxation is not merely inefficient or regrettable; it violates self-ownership.

Locke preview
1. We own ourselves.
2. Therefore we own our labor.
3. When labor is mixed with unowned things, a claim arises in those things.
4. Therefore private property can arise from self-possession and labor.

## Notation Choices
- Use `D` for a distribution, not for “desert” or “democracy.”
- Use `J(D)` only for justice-status; do not introduce a welfare function like `U(D)`.
- Use `J_{\mathrm{acq}}` and `J_{\mathrm{tr}}` for the two entitlement conditions.
- Use `\Longrightarrow` for argumentative implication and `\to` for a staged logical chain.
- Use textual predicates such as `\text{SelfOwnership}(p)` rather than dense symbolic metaphysics.
- Use `S_{\min}` and `S_{\mathrm{forbidden}}` only as editorial shorthand; label them in prose as reconstructions.
- Write money as `31\,\text{million}`, `47\,\text{million}`, `78\,\text{million}`, and `\$150` to keep arithmetic readable.
- Keep `\text{Need}` and `\text{Desert}` as conceptual labels or table headers, not as formal operators.
- Normalize names in prose and math comments to `John Stuart Mill`, `Robert Nozick`, and `John Locke`.
